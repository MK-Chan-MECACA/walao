The problem

Notifications say something is new. They don't say whether it deserves you.

A busy group produces far more messages than outcomes. What you actually need is the answer to five questions.

  • What was decided today?
  • What is waiting on my reply?
  • Which meetings, payments and deadlines were mentioned?
  • What matters, but not right now?
  • Can I find an old decision without scrolling through hundreds of messages?
How it works

Three steps, then it is a habit.

Step one

Connect a number

Pair WhatsApp by scanning a QR code, the same way WhatsApp Web works. Use a spare number — the beta note at the bottom of this page explains why that matters.

Step two

Choose the groups

Every group starts switched off. WALAO only ever receives new messages from the groups you enable, and you can turn one off again at any time.

Step three

Read one brief

At the time and timezone you pick, each group is summarised and the results merge into a single Today Brief.

Then

Ask it anything you already agreed to

“What did procurement decide yesterday?” — answered from the groups you approved and the data still inside your retention window, with the messages it relied on attached.

The sequence

What the animation is showing you.

Six beats, twenty-seven seconds, no narration. Here it is in words as well.

  1. New messages arrive from the groups you switched on.
  2. Everything else in WhatsApp is never read at all.
  3. The day's traffic is analysed group by group.
  4. Decisions, actions and dates are separated from noise.
  5. Each claim is linked back to the message it came from.
  6. One brief, five things, one minute to read.

Data custody

Built to hold less of your data, not more

  • Every claim carries the group, the time and the message it came from.
  • A group is not processed until you enable it, one at a time, on purpose.
  • Raw messages are deleted after 1 to 30 days — whichever you choose.
  • Export everything, or delete it all. Both leave an audit record.
